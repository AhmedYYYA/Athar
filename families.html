For families

What your child is actually doing here

You do not need to understand AI to help your child think clearly about it. Here is what a mission involves and what we will never do with your family.

A mission, from the outside

Your child opens a mission and works through short steps. They read one idea, then do something with it: sorting examples, spotting where AI got something wrong, or feeding it examples and watching its guess improve. When they answer, they are told why, not only whether they were right. It takes about twenty minutes and there is no way to lose.

What it teaches that a coding class will not

Children meet AI long before they meet programming. They meet it in a recommendation, a voice assistant, a homework answer that sounds certain. The skill that protects them is not writing code. It is noticing when something is a guess, checking what matters, and knowing what to keep to themselves.

Sitting with them helps, and is not required

Missions are written so a child can do them alone. But the safety lessons land harder when an adult is nearby, because one of the things we teach is to bring a trusted adult in when something matters. If you can be in the room for the first mission, be in the room.

Questions worth asking them afterwards

  • Where did you notice AI today, outside this?
  • Did it say anything you were not sure about?
  • How would you check if it was right?
  • What would you never type into it?

Questions families ask

Does my child talk to an AI?

No. There is no chat surface anywhere in ATHAR. Every screen your child sees was written and reviewed in advance. When a mission shows what an AI might say, that text is written by us as an example to examine, not generated live.

Do you collect anything about my child?

Progress is stored in your own browser and is not sent anywhere. There is no account, no email, no sign-in. Clearing your browser data clears it. We have no way to see it.

Is it safe for a seven-year-old to use alone?

Yes, by design. There is no chat, no upload, no link out to the open web from inside a mission, and nothing to buy. That said, the safety missions work better with an adult nearby, so the first one is worth doing together.

My child prefers Arabic. Is that version as good?

It is the same product, not a translation layer. Both languages are written at the same time by the same people, and a check fails our build if any English text has no Arabic counterpart. Numerals, direction and line spacing all follow the script.

What does it cost?

Nothing at the moment. This is a pilot and there is no payment system connected to it. If that changes we will say so plainly on this page before it does.
