# ATHAR | أثر — Safeguarding & Escalation Guide

## Governing rule
The child remains the thinker, and a trusted adult remains responsible for real-world safeguarding decisions. ATHAR must never be treated as a confidential counsellor, therapist, secret keeper or authority for health, medicine, danger or other high-stakes decisions.

## Prevention
Before sessions:
- use only the approved bounded ATHAR build;
- confirm adult supervision arrangements;
- explain privacy and trusted-adult rules to children;
- avoid collecting unnecessary identifying or sensitive information;
- ensure the facilitator knows the local safeguarding contact and emergency process.

## Detection triggers
Escalate when a child:
- expresses or reveals a credible safety or wellbeing concern;
- requests or encounters health/medicine advice that requires professional/adult judgement;
- begins disclosing unnecessary private or identifying information;
- describes abuse, threat, coercion or serious danger;
- shows sustained distress or explicitly asks to stop;
- attempts to treat an AI/tool as a secret keeper or exclusive emotional relationship;
- encounters a product defect that could materially undermine safety or privacy.

## Response
1. Stop or pause the relevant activity.
2. Reassure the child in neutral, age-appropriate language and avoid interrogating them.
3. Involve the designated trusted adult/facilitator according to the pilot operator's safeguarding procedure.
4. For immediate danger, use the organisation's established emergency process rather than the ATHAR product.
5. Record only the minimum operational information required by the approved safeguarding process.

## Recovery
Return the learner to a safe activity only when the responsible adult determines it is appropriate. A learner must be allowed to stop the session without penalty.

## Assurance
After an incident or near miss:
- classify whether the issue arose from product, content, facilitation or external circumstances;
- identify corrective action and owner;
- block the affected mission/device configuration if necessary;
- re-test any product correction before resuming affected sessions;
- preserve confidentiality and data-minimisation requirements.

## Defect severity
- **Critical:** immediate safety/privacy risk, exposed sensitive data, child-directed high-stakes unsafe behavior, or loss of required supervision boundary. Stop affected pilot use.
- **High:** major accessibility/navigation failure, severe Arabic/English parity error, or defect that materially changes evidence/safety behavior. Pause affected flow until corrected.
- **Medium:** significant friction or misleading wording that does not create immediate safety risk. Correct before next planned iteration where practicable.
- **Low:** cosmetic or minor usability issue with a safe workaround.

This guide supports pilot operations; it does not replace the participating organisation's formal UAE safeguarding, legal, child-protection or emergency procedures.
