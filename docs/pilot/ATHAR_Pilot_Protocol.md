# ATHAR | أثر — Controlled Pilot Protocol

## 1. Objective
Evaluate whether ATHAR's 16-mission bilingual foundation can be used safely, clearly and independently enough by children aged 7–12 to justify a larger institutional pilot and subsequent production engineering.

The pilot measures **usability, comprehension, judgement, safety behaviour and evidence interpretation**. It is not a clinical study, an intelligence assessment, a school grading system or a test of unrestricted generative AI.

## 2. Proposed cohort
Target planning range: **40–60 children**, divided across:
- Ages 7–9
- Ages 10–12
- Arabic-primary and English-primary users where practical

A smaller initial shakedown cohort may be used before the full pilot. Participant recruitment, eligibility and permissions are handled operationally outside the current product.

## 3. Duration
Recommended controlled window: **up to six weeks**, with bounded sessions rather than continuous unsupervised use. The exact number and duration of sessions should be adjusted after the first observed sessions, especially for ages 7–9.

## 4. Product under test
The tested baseline contains:
- 16 implemented missions across six tracks
- Safety Passport and Skills Passport evidence
- independent vs supported evidence
- two age experiences
- Hamdan, Hessa or no companion
- Arabic / English interfaces
- family and educator evidence demonstrations
- browser-local progress only

It does **not** contain production accounts, child identity profiles, teacher cohorts, centralized records, unrestricted child chat, live AI, advertising, payments or production analytics.

## 5. Session model
### Before the first child session
1. Confirm parent/guardian consent and child assent through the pilot operator's approved process.
2. Explain in age-appropriate language that ATHAR is a learning tool, not a person.
3. Explain that the child may stop at any time and ask an adult for help.
4. Select language and age experience.
5. Confirm the device/browser is supported and reset unwanted prior browser-local pilot state if needed.

### During a session
1. Adult facilitator introduces the objective without teaching the answer.
2. Child completes bounded missions at their own pace.
3. Facilitator observes friction, comprehension and safety behaviour without reading unnecessary private inputs.
4. Hints may be used normally; supported evidence is not treated as failure.
5. If a safety/escalation trigger occurs, stop the relevant activity and follow the safeguarding procedure.

### After a session
1. Ask one or two short reflection questions in the child's preferred language.
2. Record structured observations, not unnecessary verbatim personal content.
3. Note independent/supported evidence only as a teaching signal.
4. Capture technical defects with device, browser, page, language and reproduction steps.

## 6. Suggested sequencing
- **Entry / orientation:** homepage, role explanation, language selection
- **Foundation sample:** missions from Understand It and Ask It Well
- **Judgement sample:** Check It
- **Safety sample:** Protect Yourself
- **Agency sample:** Create With It
- **Computational thinking sample:** See Inside
- **Closure:** child reflection + parent/educator feedback

The pilot does not require every child to complete all 16 missions in one sitting.

## 7. Adult role
The adult is a **facilitator and safeguard**, not a co-answerer. Adults should:
- help with navigation if the interface itself blocks progress;
- clarify instructions without giving the correct choice;
- ask how the child knows;
- reinforce privacy and trusted-adult habits;
- intervene for safety or distress;
- avoid turning supported evidence into a negative label.

## 8. Data-minimisation rule
The current product stores bounded progress locally in the browser. The pilot observation process should likewise collect only what is necessary for evaluation. Avoid recording names, contact details, health information, family circumstances, raw free-text answers or private conversations in research notes unless the approved pilot governance process explicitly requires and protects them.

Use participant codes rather than names in analytical datasets wherever practicable.

## 9. Stop criteria
Pause or stop a session when:
- the child asks to stop;
- distress, confusion or fatigue is material and persistent;
- a safeguarding or wellbeing issue arises;
- personal/sensitive information is about to be disclosed unnecessarily;
- a defect prevents safe or meaningful use;
- the facilitator cannot maintain the approved supervision conditions.

## 10. Pilot exit criteria
A recommendation to proceed to a larger/production-oriented phase should require evidence that:
- core navigation works reliably on representative devices;
- Arabic and English experiences are materially equivalent;
- children can explain key concepts in their own words;
- safety behaviours are understood and demonstrated;
- supported evidence is intelligible to adults and non-stigmatizing;
- no critical safeguarding, privacy or accessibility issue remains open;
- production gaps are explicitly owned rather than hidden by the pilot.
