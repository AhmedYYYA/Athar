Safety

How safety is built, not claimed

Anyone can write a safety page. These are the specific decisions that produced this product, including the ones that cost us features.

Six things this will never do

  • Open a chatbot to a child Children work inside missions that were written and reviewed, never a blank box that answers anything.
  • Sell them anything No advertising, no sponsored content, no behaviour tracked to target them later.
  • Pretend to be their friend Hamdan and Hessa point something out and then get out of the way. They never ask for affection or secrecy.
  • Keep a secret from a trusted adult Nothing here encourages a child to hide what they are doing. The opposite is taught directly.
  • Score or profile a learner No automated diagnosis, no ranking, no behavioural profile built from how a child plays.
  • Call a hint mastery Taking help is fine and it is recorded as help. A teacher sees the difference.

Why there is no chatbot

An open text box that answers anything is the single riskiest thing you can put in front of a child, and it is also the easiest thing to build. Every screen here was written in advance and reviewed. When a mission shows what an AI might say, we wrote that line deliberately so the child can examine it. Nothing is generated while a child is using the product.

Why the companions stay small

Hamdan and Hessa exist to point something out and then step back. They do not ask how the child feels, do not remember them between sessions, do not build a relationship, and do not ask for anything to be kept between the two of them. A child can also turn them off entirely and work alone. That option is offered as an equal, not buried.

What is stored, and where

Which missions were finished, how many traces were earned, whether a hint was used, and which companion was chosen. All of it sits in the browser on the device the child used. None of it is transmitted. There is no account, no email address, no analytics script and no advertising network anywhere on this site.

Teaching children to involve an adult

One of the safety exercises shows an AI telling a child they do not need to tell a grown-up. The child is asked what to do, and the answer that earns the mission is to bring in a trusted adult anyway. Language that isolates a child from adults is a pattern worth naming early, and this is the age to name it.

What we have not solved

There is no read-aloud audio yet, which matters for the youngest children and for anyone who reads slowly. Fonts currently load from a third party, which logs visitor addresses, and that contradicts what this page says about third-party requests until we host them ourselves. Classroom reporting does not exist, and building it will mean handling data we currently do not touch. We would rather list these than let the page imply they are done.

What this was built against

The design follows child digital-safety guidance used in the UAE together with international frameworks from UNESCO and UNICEF on children and artificial intelligence. Those documents inform the decisions above; they do not certify this product, and we do not claim any endorsement.
